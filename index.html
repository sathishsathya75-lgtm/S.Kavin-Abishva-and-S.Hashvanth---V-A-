How to explore

Open the same website from your existing QR. Choose Start camera AR, allow camera access and aim at the whole Hiro marker. Use a printed marker or show it on another screen.

Open the official Hiro marker

One finger rotates the model. Two fingers zoom. Solar and Lunar set the correct alignments. Play moves the Moon; the slider lets you position it yourself. Look closer isolates a body so its surface is easy to inspect.

What is real imagery?

Earth: NASA Blue Marble satellite-based global surface mosaic. Moon: NASA LRO / CGI Moon Kit color map. These are texture maps wrapped onto 3D spheres, not live feeds. The texture status tells you which source actually loaded.

Sun: Solar System Scope's artistic solar texture, with a simulated glow. Its orange color and apparent surface motion are visual illustrations, not live solar observations.

Scientific limits

Sizes, distances and orbital speed are simplified so all three bodies fit above a table. The orbit is kept level to demonstrate alignments; the real Moon's tilted orbit means eclipses do not happen every month. Close-up mode lights a body for inspection and hides the other bodies; return to All to see eclipse shadows.

In All view, shadow darkness is calculated from how much of the displayed Sun is hidden by the other sphere. The Moon's red tint is a teaching effect, not a calculation of Earth's atmosphere. In a real total lunar eclipse, sunlight filtered and bent by Earth's atmosphere can make the Moon look reddish.

Camera privacy: marker tracking happens on your device. This page does not record or upload camera images. Its libraries and surface images are downloaded from their hosting sites.

When a texture fails to load

Check the internet connection and press Retry. A school network may block an image host. You can also load a saved equirectangular map below. The included optional downloader makes a local assets folder for hosting these files yourself.

Safety: keep the camera pointed at your marker, not the real Sun.
